# Limitations and non-claims

- B1 has not been deciphered; no complete readable plaintext or reversible
  treasure-location message was recovered.
- B2's advertised plaintext is used to reconstruct constraints. This is not an
  independent decryption.
- The local deletion is not uniquely identifiable. Five words remain possible.
- Four observed B2 number types are internally conflicted; the output preserves
  those conflicts.
- Sparse printed labels require interpolation and collision policies.
- Later table discontinuities prevent projecting one simple shift through the
  entire Declaration.
- The alphabet-walk statistic is post hoc because its motivating passage was
  already known.
- The equality-pattern null is one explicit chance mechanism, not a model of
  every authentic or fraudulent construction process.
- The Monte Carlo result is not a posterior probability of fraud.
- Optimized neighboring-number edits are sensitivity demonstrations, not
  evidence for a longer intended alphabet.
- Historical arguments summarized from [Nickell and Kruh](prior_art.md#19821988--joe-nickell-and-louis-kruh)
  favor Ward as a suspect but do not uniquely prove authorship or an exact
  fabrication date.
- Matyas's complete 1996 monograph, King's full 1993 paper, and the archival
  source behind the Remington/Hill webpage were not inspected. Priority claims
  are therefore provisional and deliberately narrow; each access limitation is
  identified at the relevant entry in the [prior-art chronology](prior_art.md).
