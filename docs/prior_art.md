# Prior art chronology

This chronology separates inspected primary or full-text sources from claims
available only through abstracts, later authorial accounts, or secondary
witnesses. It is designed to prevent the project from absorbing earlier work.

## 1885 — the pamphlet

The anonymous narrative and three ciphertexts appeared in James B. Ward's
*The Beale Papers*. Its printed Declaration includes `pursuing (240)`; the word
is not missing from the pamphlet page. Our rejected omission theory concerned
an inferred working enumeration behind B2. [Scan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Beale_Papers.djvu)

## 1979 — Carl Hammer

Hammer analyzed B2's known-plaintext construction and clerical corrections
before the B1 alphabet anomaly was published. He bracketed the separate question
of authenticity. [NSA-declassified compilation, pp. 34–41](https://media.defense.gov/2021/Jul/15/2002763573/-1/-1/0/DOC656733.PDF)

## 1980 — James J. Gillogly

Gillogly decoded B1 with Declaration initials and published the raw passage
`ABFDEFGHIIJKLMMNOHPP`. He considered chance, another cipher layer, and
fabrication; he favored fabrication without presenting it as proof. He did not
recover B1 plaintext. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/0161-118091854979) ·
[public reproduction](https://www.angelfire.com/pro/bealeciphers/Graphics/DissentingOpinion.pdf)

## 1982 — Dorothy E. Denning

Denning reproduced Gillogly's sequence, discussed likely errors, and proposed
the regularized form `ABCDEFGHIIJKLMMNOOPP`. This was an intended-form proposal,
not the later piecewise B2-table reconstruction. [Catalog and author-hosted PDF](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp76346)

## 1982–1988 — Joe Nickell and Louis Kruh

Nickell developed a complementary historical case based on chronology,
language, provenance, and stylistic comparison, with Ward as the leading
suspect. Kruh's accessible contemporary synthesis combines Nickell's case with
additional analysis. Neither result is a B1 solution or proof of unique
authorship. Nickell's original article remains access-restricted, so details in
this repository are limited to its bibliographic record, Kruh's inspected
summary, and Nickell's later restatements.

- [Nickell 1982 JSTOR record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4248566)
- [Kruh 1988 inspected reproduction](https://www.angelfire.com/pro/bealeciphers/Graphics/Bamboozlement2.pdf)
- [Kruh 1988 NSA copy](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/beale-papers/doc656729.pdf)
- [Nickell 2000 later account](https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2000/03/22164857/p14.pdf)

## 1989 — E. E. Remington / Jeffrey A. Hill

An accessible but bibliographically ambiguous web reproduction is titled for
E. E. Remington and contains an article signed Jeffrey A. Hill, dated 29 May
1989. It prints a piecewise B2 table, leaves entries 242–245 indeterminate, and
gives `ABCDEFGHIIJKLMMNOHPP`. It is prior art for the corrected run and local
discontinuity. No physical or archival original was located.
[Web reproduction](https://thomasbeale.tripod.com/52EERemington.htm)

## 1993 — John C. King

King's inspected publisher abstract states that reconstructing the B2 key
reveals stronger statistical anomalies in B1 than Gillogly reported. The full
paper was not inspected, so this project makes no narrower claim about its
contents. [DOI and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/0161-119391867971)

## 1996 and later — Stephen M. Matyas Jr.

Later author-controlled pages, corroborated by Nick Pelling's summary, place an
accidental one-word omission after 241 and before 246 and say the exact word is
not recoverable. Matyas's privately printed 1996 monograph was not inspected;
our five-model reduction is therefore described as reproducible and apparently
undocumented in the sources examined, not categorically first.

- [How Cipher No. 2 Was Decoded](https://www.bealetreasurestory.com/id26.html)
- [B1 and B3 are Genuine Ciphers](https://www.bealetreasurestory.com/id18.html)
- [Pelling's Matyas summary](https://ciphermysteries.com/2018/12/16/more-notes-on-the-beale-ciphers)

## 2013 — Todd D. Mateer

Mateer reverse-engineered B2 from its advertised plaintext, documenting
peculiar enumeration errors including `self-evident`, an extra `a`, duplicated
480, and later intervals near 630 and 670. This is known-plaintext key
reconstruction, not independent decryption. The inspected paper does not name
`pursuing`. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2013.798517) ·
[public PDF](https://zodiackillerciphers.com/2013-3-04-Beale-2-BIT.pdf)

## 2010–2018 — Nick Pelling

Pelling revisited neighboring-number repairs and in 2018 printed the corrected
20-character result with exact mappings. His later post summarizes Matyas's
interval. These are required prior art despite being research-blog posts.

- [2010 sequence revisit](https://ciphermysteries.com/2010/06/22/jim-gilloglys-beale-sequence-revisited)
- [2013 Gillogly strings revisit](https://ciphermysteries.com/2013/11/21/beale-ciphers-gillogly-strings-revisited)
- [2018 exact corrected mappings](https://ciphermysteries.com/2018/11/15/refining-beale-cipher-b1s-cipher-table)
- [2018 Matyas synthesis](https://ciphermysteries.com/2018/12/16/more-notes-on-the-beale-ciphers)

## Priority boundary

We do not claim discovery of the alphabet sequence, corrected table, one-word
displacement, or fabrication hypothesis. The project's defensible contribution
is the explicit rejection of `pursuing`, the five-model ambiguity, invariance
and complete-position demonstration, deterministic conflict-preserving
alignment, and modern reproducible controls. Remaining access gaps are listed
in [limitations](limitations.md).
